## 1. Executive Summary

Phase 3 established complete mathematical equation parity between the canonical simulator outcome model and the model-aligned target controller, eliminating structural equation discrepancies. However, empirical analysis revealed residual prediction gaps between the controller's predicted clean success probability $\hat{p}$ and the simulator's latent clean probability $p_{\text{clean}}$ (e.g., $-0.88\text{ pp}$ in `base_heterogeneous`, $-2.89\text{ pp}$ in `reliability_stress`, $+4.48\text{ pp}$ in `temporal_dynamics`, and $+8.84\text{ pp}$ in `distribution_shift`).

Phase 4 investigated the causes of these residual gaps through a rigorous three-stage experimental campaign ($N = 540,000$ interaction events across 5 scenarios, 9 random seeds, and 4 principled controller ablations):
1. **Secondary Skill Omission**: In Phase 3, `visual_discrimination` (present as a secondary skill in 5 of 9 games, but never primary) received zero practice updates and decayed towards $0.05$. Introducing a population-prior-based secondary transfer update ($\Delta \hat{\theta}_{\text{sec}} = 0.30 \cdot \mu_{\text{lr}} \cdot (0.95 - \hat{\theta}_{\text{sec}})$) reduced secondary mastery estimation MAE by $23.3\%$ (from $0.1426$ to $0.1094$) and eliminated the secondary mastery underestimation bias (from $-0.0814$ to $+0.0109$).
2. **Observation Corruption**: Sensor slips ($P(\text{slip} \mid \text{corruption}) = 0.85$) severely dragged down mastery beliefs in standard and high-noise scenarios. Implementing a Bayesian soft-label observation update $\tilde{y} = P(y_{\text{clean}} = 1 \mid y_{\text{obs}}, \hat{\rho}_t)$ using pre-outcome observable reliability $\hat{\rho}_t$ shrunk the prediction gap in `reliability_stress` from $-2.92\text{ pp}$ to $-0.86\text{ pp}$ and reduced $D_{\min}$ bound saturation from $52.2\%$ to $26.8\%$.
3. **The Limits of Robustness Under Shift**: Under `distribution_shift` (initial mastery $\mu = 0.25$ vs prior $0.45$) and `temporal_dynamics` (forgetting rate $\mu = 0.07$ vs prior $0.02$), dampening the penalty of observed failures prevented the controller from rapidly discovering that the learner was weaker than the population prior. Consequently, the prediction gap widened to $+11.82\text{ pp}$ (reliability-aware) and $+13.18\text{ pp}$ (combined). **Equation alignment and sensor noise filtering cannot compensate for unmodeled distribution shift in latent priors without dynamic prior adaptation.**
4. **Reproducibility and Integrity**: All 51 deterministic tests pass in $29.90\text{s}$, verifying strict absence of simulator truth leakage, proper event ordering, and complete self-containment.

## 3. Confirmed Code Findings vs. Hypotheses

### Finding A: The Secondary-Skill Asymmetry
In `configs/default.yaml`, the 6 skills and 9 games exhibit a structural asymmetry:
- `visual_discrimination` is configured as a secondary skill in **5 out of 9 games**:
  1. `trace_glow` (primary: `fine_motor`)
  2. `bubble_pop_brigade` (primary: `fine_motor`)
  3. `category_sorting` (primary: `categorization`)
  4. `visual_flip_match` (primary: `working_memory`)
  5. `pattern_train` (primary: `sequential_reasoning`)
- `visual_discrimination` is the primary skill of **zero** games in the registry.

**Mechanism**: In the Phase 3 baseline updater, `observe_outcome` updated only `self.estimated_mastery[prim_skill]`. In `select_next_task`, forgetting decay was applied to all skills. Because `visual_discrimination` never served as primary, it received zero practice reinforcement and steadily decayed toward $0.05$. In contrast, the virtual learner simulator continuously awarded $30\%$ transfer practice gains. Effective mastery $\theta_{\text{eff}} = 0.85 \hat{\theta}_{\text{prim}} + 0.15 \hat{\theta}_{\text{sec}}$ was systematically depressed, causing the controller to underestimate clean success probability.

### Finding B: Asymmetric Observation Corruption
In `src/grow21_simulation_benchmark/simulator.py` (lines 167-175):
- Physical corruption flips clean successes into observed failures with probability **$0.85$** (motor tremor slips, acoustic cutoffs).
- Accidental hits flip clean failures into observed successes with probability **$0.04$**.

**Mechanism**: In noisy scenarios (`reliability_stress`), observed failure was treated by the baseline updater as a failure of skill, dragging belief down by $8\%$ toward $0.05$. In reality, $85\%$ of corrupted successes were false failures. This induced a severe negative belief bias ($\text{bias} = -0.0624$), forcing $52.7\%$ of trials into lower-bound saturation ($D = 0.08$).

## 4. Estimator Variants and Assumptions

Four controller variants were implemented in `src/grow21_simulation_benchmark/closed_loop.py` under identical common random seeds and identical virtual learner initial profiles:

### 1. `model_aligned_baseline` (Phase 3 Baseline)
- **Primary update**: Hard heuristic step based on observed outcome:
  $$\Delta \hat{\theta}_{\text{prim}} = \begin{cases} +0.08 \cdot (0.95 - \hat{\theta}_{\text{prim}}), & \text{if } y_{\text{obs}} = 1 \\ -0.08 \cdot (\hat{\theta}_{\text{prim}} - 0.05), & \text{if } y_{\text{obs}} = 0 \end{cases}$$
- **Secondary update**: None.
- **Assumptions**: Observations are uncorrupted; secondary skills do not experience practice transfer.

### 2. `transfer_aware`
- **Primary update**: Identical to baseline heuristic.
- **Secondary update**: Tracks partial secondary transfer gains using the declared population learning rate prior ($\mu_{\text{lr}} = 0.08$) and transfer ratio ($0.30$):
  $$\Delta \hat{\theta}_{\text{sec}} = 0.30 \cdot \mu_{\text{lr}} \cdot (0.95 - \hat{\theta}_{\text{sec}}) = 0.024 \cdot (0.95 - \hat{\theta}_{\text{sec}})$$
- **Assumptions**: Virtual learner population prior learning rate is $0.08$; secondary transfer ratio is $0.30$. Does not inspect the individual learner's true learning rate or true mastery.

### 3. `reliability_aware`
- **Primary update**: Computes the posterior expected clean outcome $\tilde{y} = P(y_{\text{clean}} = 1 \mid y_{\text{obs}}, \hat{\rho}_t)$ using pre-outcome observable estimated reliability $\hat{\rho}_t \in [0, 1]$ and prior predicted clean probability $p = \hat{p}_t$:
  $$\text{If } y_{\text{obs}} = 1: \quad \tilde{y} = \frac{p \cdot (\hat{\rho}_t + 0.15(1 - \hat{\rho}_t))}{p \cdot (\hat{\rho}_t + 0.15(1 - \hat{\rho}_t)) + (1 - p) \cdot (0.04(1 - \hat{\rho}_t))}$$
  $$\text{If } y_{\text{obs}} = 0: \quad \tilde{y} = \frac{p \cdot (0.85(1 - \hat{\rho}_t))}{p \cdot (0.85(1 - \hat{\rho}_t)) + (1 - p) \cdot (\hat{\rho}_t + 0.96(1 - \hat{\rho}_t))}$$
  $$\text{Target mastery: } \theta_{\text{target}} = \tilde{y} \cdot 0.95 + (1 - \tilde{y}) \cdot 0.05$$
  $$\Delta \hat{\theta}_{\text{prim}} = \mu_{\text{lr}} \cdot (\theta_{\text{target}} - \hat{\theta}_{\text{prim}})$$
- **Secondary update**: None.
- **Assumptions**: Sensor reliability estimates $\hat{\rho}_t$ reflect physical corruption risk; physical slip probability is $0.85$; false contact probability is $0.04$.

### 4. `combined_transfer_reliability`
- Jointly applies the Bayesian reliability-aware soft-label primary update and the population-prior secondary transfer update.

## 6. Paired Policy Differences and Uncertainty Analysis

Paired differences on the identical virtual learners (with $95\%$ bootstrap confidence intervals across learner clusters, $B = 500$) establish the exact effect of the belief updates:

### In `reliability_stress`:
- **Probability Absolute Error Reduction**:
  - `transfer_aware`: $-0.0039$ ($95\%\text{ CI}: [-0.0042, -0.0036]$)
  - `reliability_aware`: $-0.0061$ ($95\%\text{ CI}: [-0.0068, -0.0055]$)
  - `combined`: $-0.0057$ ($95\%\text{ CI}: [-0.0066, -0.0048]$)
- **Reduction in Lower-Bound Saturation ($D_{\min}$)**:
  - `transfer_aware`: $-10.64\text{ pp}$
  - `reliability_aware`: $-25.37\text{ pp}$
  - `combined`: $-34.00\text{ pp}$ (from $52.2\%$ down to $18.2\%$)

### In `base_heterogeneous`:
- Secondary skill (`visual_discrimination`) mastery estimation error:
  - Baseline signed bias: **$-0.0814$** (systematic underestimation)
  - Transfer-aware signed bias: **$+0.0109$** (bias eliminated)
  - Secondary MAE reduced from $0.1426$ to $0.1094$ ($p < 10^{-15}$).

### In `distribution_shift` (The Cost of Reliability Under Prior Shift):
- Under distribution shift, initial true mastery is $\mu = 0.25$, but the controller starts with prior $0.45$.
- Baseline heuristic: Observed failures drag mastery down toward $0.05$. This downward drag accidentally accelerates convergence toward the low true state.
- Reliability-aware updater: Knowing that sensor reliability is low, the update attributes failures to motor noise rather than low skill, preventing the rapid downward correction.
- Paired probability error change: $+0.0212$ ($95\%\text{ CI}: [+0.0208, +0.0216]$).
- Primary mastery MAE increases by $+0.0887$.

## 9. Limitations and Recommended Next Experiments

1. **Static Initial Prior Under Shift**: The largest remaining prediction gap occurs under `distribution_shift` ($+13.18\text{ pp}$), where the controller's static prior ($\hat{\theta}_0 = 0.45$) conflicts with the cohort's true initial ability ($\mu = 0.25$). A fixed population prior cannot adapt to cohorts with distinct developmental baselines.
   - *Recommended Phase 5 Experiment*: Implement an empirical Bayes initial calibration phase (e.g., first 5 trials of broad-spectrum screening tasks) to estimate a learner-specific initial mastery prior before launching closed-loop target control.
2. **Fixed Forgetting Rate Prior**: In `temporal_dynamics`, learners forget at rate $\lambda = 0.07$, but the controller's prior is fixed at $0.02$. Estimating forgetting rate from post-break recovery trajectories would close the temporal prediction gap.
3. **Noisy vs Ground Truth Reliability Telemetry**: The current likelihood model uses the observable uncertainty estimate $\hat{\rho}_t$. In scenarios with severe sensor dropout, fallback default uncertainties slightly underestimate physical corruption risk.
